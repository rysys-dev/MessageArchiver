Your messages, kept for good, in files you own

MessageArchiver copies your Messages history (iMessage, SMS and RCS) to a folder on your Mac, with every photo, video and voice note. It reads it from Messages on your Mac or, if you don't use Messages there, from a backup of your iPhone. The archive opens in any browser, on any computer, with no Apple account. Its Message Explorer shows your people and conversations side by side, like a messaging app.

Automatic updates keep it current, daily or weekly. If Messages keeps messages for only 30 days or a year, or a message is deleted on the phone, the archive already has it: an update only adds, and never loses what the archive holds.

Find any message

Search with words in any order, or an exact phrase, and narrow by person, conversation, dates or attachments. Click a result to see it in its conversation. Before you export, you can also find conversations by what was said in them.

Every app, one conversation per person

Plug-ins add other apps' messages beside Messages: WhatsApp, Signal and Telegram, and Instagram, Messenger, Google Voice, Tinder and Hinge (in testing). Someone you text and also reach on WhatsApp is one person and one conversation, and each message shows how it was sent. Plug-ins are signed by RYSYS and run with no network access.

Words in photos and voice notes

Search can also find the words in your photos (a receipt's total, a screenshot, a sign) and what your voice notes say. macOS reads them on your Mac, and nothing is sent anywhere. It's off until you turn it on.

Your iPhone's whole history

A backup of your iPhone, made on your Mac with Finder, often goes further back than your Mac, and has WhatsApp's whole history too. Encrypted backups work: the password is used only to read the backup and is never saved.

Ready for other software

Every conversation is also saved as Markdown and JSON, for an AI assistant, a spreadsheet or your own tools. Attachments are named by date, and photos, voice notes and videos can be converted so any browser plays them.

Proof nothing changed

Every archive has a SHA-256 manifest of its files, and Verify Archive checks each one, so you can show the archive is exactly as it was made.

Legal transcripts, for a court or a lawyer

Turn on Legal tools for formal transcripts in PDF and HTML: numbered entries, Bates numbers, Page X of Y, and attachments listed as exhibits with their SHA-256. Make an exhibit of only the messages you choose, with every gap marked, and redact it, with a redaction log and an unredacted copy beside it.

Free and open source (GNU GPL v3). Fully offline: it makes no network connections. Read only: it never changes Messages, an iPhone backup or another app's data. For Macs with Apple silicon, on macOS 27 or later.
