MessageArchiver

Export your Messages conversations, with their photos, videos and voice notes, to a folder on your Mac.

This is the short guide. The full manual (PDF, 39 pages, 6.4 MB) covers every screen and setting with screenshots, and has a chapter on legal transcripts.

Install

You need a Mac with Apple silicon (M1 or later) running macOS 27 or later.

  1. Download the zip and double-click it. You get MessageArchiver.app.
  2. Drag it to your Applications folder.
  3. Double-click it. macOS says it can't check the app for malicious software, because it isn't from the App Store or a registered Apple developer. Click Done.
  4. Open System Settings › Privacy & Security, scroll down to the message about MessageArchiver, and click Open Anyway. Enter your Mac's password.
  5. Open MessageArchiver again and click Open Anyway. You only do this once.

To install a newer version, quit MessageArchiver, replace the app in Applications, and open it. Your settings and permissions stay.

First launch

Full Disk Access

macOS keeps your Messages history private, so MessageArchiver asks for Full Disk Access before it can read it. The first screen has a button that opens the right place in System Settings: turn on MessageArchiver there. When macOS offers Quit & Reopen, click it.

Contacts (optional)

With Contacts allowed, conversations show people's names instead of phone numbers and email addresses. You can skip this and turn it on later in Settings.

The start screen

The start screen shows what's in Messages: how many messages and conversations, the dates they span, and the attachments. Click Choose What to Export.

Choose what to export

The dashboard lists every conversation, newest first. Everything is ticked to begin with.

  • Dates: All, Last 12 Months, or Custom (from and to, both days included).
  • Conversations or People: tick conversations one by one, or tick a person to take all their conversations.
  • Everyone, In Contacts, Not in Contacts: narrows the list, and what's exported, to people in your Contacts or not.
  • All platforms (at the right of the dates): a tick for each way messages were sent, such as iMessage, SMS, RCS or WhatsApp. Untick one to leave its messages out of the lists, the counts and the export, even inside a conversation that mixes them; the menu then says how many are ticked, for example "3 of 4 platforms". The archive says which platforms it holds.
  • Leave out group chats (in People): only one-to-one conversations.
  • Search: in Conversations, finds names, phone numbers, emails and words said in the messages; each row says how many messages mention it. In People, it finds names and numbers. The checkbox at the top of the list ticks every row you see, or unticks them all when they all are; it shows a dash when only some are ticked.

The right side shows, first, what doesn't change as you choose: All sources, each source's totals, and the people with the most messages (click a name to see that person in People). Then what you've chosen: a chart of its activity by month, and This export: how many conversations, messages and attachments you've chosen, and roughly how much space the archive needs. Save to shows where it goes and the free space there.

The first time, MessageArchiver reads every message once so the search can find words said (a line under the search box shows its progress). After that it only reads what's new.

People and names

A person is all their phone numbers and email addresses together. MessageArchiver groups them from your Contacts and from Messages itself. In People you can fix what it got wrong:

  • Merge… two or more people who are really one, under one name.
  • Split a person back into one row per number or email.
  • Rename… a person, for example someone not in your Contacts.

These are kept for every export and only change the archive, never Messages or Contacts.

A phone number or email is the same person in every app, so someone you text and also reach on WhatsApp is one person, and one conversation. A name or username another app uses (a WhatsApp id without a number, for example) stays that app's own until you Merge… it with the right person. A transcript says which of a person's numbers and names were joined by hand.

Export

Click Export…. Name the archive (the suggestion says what's in it) and choose:

FormatWhat it is
Conversation pages (HTML)Looks like Messages, with photos and videos in place. Opens in any browser. Your own messages carry the name set for transcripts (and the number or email they were sent from, when Messages records it); in a conversation over several apps, each message says how it was sent.
MarkdownPlain text, one file per conversation: for reading, or to give an AI assistant.
JSONStructured data for research and other software.
Legal transcript (PDF and HTML)Numbered entries, attachments listed as exhibits with their SHA-256, Bates numbers and Page X of Y. Add the matter, who prepared it and for whom; they go on the cover page.
  • Attachments: a folder for each person or group, or all in one folder.
  • Convert photos, audio and video so any browser plays them: HEIC photos become JPEG, voice notes M4A, and MOV videos MP4. Videos need the free ffmpeg tool; without it they're copied as they are (Safari still plays them).
  • Keep the originals beside converted copies: recommended for a legal record.

The export runs with a progress bar; you can keep using your Mac. Stop leaves complete pages for what was done. When it finishes, open the archive, show it in Finder, verify it or search it.

What's in an archive

Each export is a new folder; nothing already on the disk is replaced.

ItemWhat it holds
index.htmlStart here: what was read, and every conversation with its counts, its sources (Messages, Signal…), its dates and links to each format. Type in its box to find a conversation.
attachment-notes.htmlAttachments that are missing, couldn't be copied, or were kept in their original format, by conversation; linked from the index. Only when there are some.
Conversations/The conversation pages.
Transcripts/PDF and HTML transcripts, and the Bates ranges.
Markdown/, JSON/One file per conversation; JSON/ also has people.json and archive.json.
Attachments/Photos, videos, voice notes and files, named by date, each dated as it was sent.
manifest.json, SHA256SUMS.txtThe size and SHA-256 of every file, to prove nothing changed.

If another app's messages couldn't be read during an export (see Other apps), the rest is exported, and the conversations that draw on that app are marked incomplete in the index, on their transcripts and in archive.json. Updating the archive later fills them in.

Missing attachments are ones the messages name but whose file wasn't where they were read from (for Messages, on the Mac); they're listed in attachment-notes.html, and each conversation's count in the index links there. With Messages in iCloud, older attachments can stay in iCloud: open the conversation in Messages to download them, then export again.

Update an archive

File › Update Archive… (⇧⌘U), or Update an existing archive… on the dashboard, adds what's new since the archive was made, with the same choices. Only conversations with new or edited messages are rebuilt. The files it replaces go to the Trash (or, on a drive without one, to a RYSYS Trash folder on that drive).

Verify an archive

File › Verify Archive… checks every file against the manifest and lists any that changed, went missing or were added. Anyone can check it without MessageArchiver too: in Terminal, in the archive folder, run shasum -a 256 -c SHA256SUMS.txt.

Search an archive

File › Search an Archive… (⇧⌘F) finds any message in an archive. Type words in any order (accents and case don't matter; put a phrase in quotes), and narrow by person, conversation, dates or attachments. Click a result to see it in its conversation, outlined.

iPhone backups

MessageArchiver can read a backup of an iPhone made with Finder instead of this Mac's Messages, which helps when the iPhone has older messages than the Mac.

  1. Connect the iPhone, select it in Finder's sidebar, and click Back Up Now with "Back up all of the data on your iPhone to this Mac" chosen.
  2. In MessageArchiver, open the menu under the heading on the start screen (or in the dashboard's toolbar) and choose the backup.
  3. If the backup is encrypted, enter its password. It's used only to read the backup and is never saved; you enter it again next time.

The backup is only read. With a backup, the dashboard's search covers names and numbers only, since a backup's messages aren't copied anywhere.

With a backup chosen, plug-ins that can read backups (WhatsApp) read that backup too, since the phone keeps the whole history; the menu's Also read says so, for example "WhatsApp in “Alex’s iPhone” backup".

Other apps: WhatsApp, Signal, Telegram and plug-ins

Messages is built in. Other apps' messages are read by plug-ins, which you download from MessageArchiver's page only if you use that app.

  1. Download the plug-in (a .maplugin file) and double-click it, or choose File › Install Plug-in….
  2. MessageArchiver asks first, then installs it, if RYSYS signed it and nothing in it was changed since. Anything else is refused, with the reason.
  3. The source menu (under the heading, or in the dashboard's toolbar) now has Also read: each app found, ticked. Untick one to leave it out.

Someone you reach in two apps is one conversation, marked for example "Messages + WhatsApp", each message with how it was sent. Group chats stay separate. Manage Sources… in the same menu opens Settings at Sources: each plug-in, what it reads, its Instructions and Remove….

If a plug-in can't read its app (it stopped, or didn't answer), a notice says so with Try Again; everything else works as before. An updated plug-in from the same page usually fixes it.

WhatsApp

The WhatsApp plug-in reads WhatsApp for Mac's chats, or your iPhone's, from a Finder backup. WhatsApp on a Mac holds only what arrived after it was linked, and attachments only once they were opened there; a backup has the phone's whole history. One-to-one chats and groups are read; status updates and broadcast lists are not. Someone WhatsApp knows only by a hidden id, not a number, shows under the name they gave WhatsApp.

Signal

Signal isn't in iPhone backups, so the Signal plug-in reads the chat history Signal Desktop exports. In Signal Desktop, open Settings (the gear, lower left) › Chats › Export chat history and press Export: a folder named signal-export- and the date appears in Downloads. Then choose Signal: Choose an Export… in the source menu (or Choose… in Settings › Sources), and choose that folder, or Downloads to read the newest export in it. For a later update, export again: only what is new is added. One-to-one chats, groups and Note to Self are read, with reactions, replies, every version of an edited message, calls and group changes. Signal Desktop holds only what it got since it was linked, and the phone may not have sent it older photos; those are listed as missing. The export folder holds your messages unencrypted: keep it safe, or delete it once your archive is made.

Telegram

Telegram keeps your chats on its servers, so the Telegram plug-in reads the copy Telegram gives you: in Telegram for Mac, Settings › Data and Storage › Export Telegram Data; in Telegram Desktop, Settings › Advanced › Export Telegram data. Set Format to Machine-readable JSON (or Both: HTML alone can't be read) and Folder to Downloads (Telegram for Mac otherwise saves inside its own folder, where MessageArchiver can't look). The first export can take about a day to be ready. Then choose Telegram: Choose an Export… in the source menu, and the export's folder, or Downloads. One-to-one chats, groups, bots and Saved Messages are read; channels are not. Telegram's export has phone numbers only in your contacts, so a person is matched to Contacts by their number when their name matches one of your contacts, and otherwise listed by Telegram's id. An edited message is marked edited; Telegram doesn't keep its earlier versions. The export holds your messages unencrypted: keep it safe, or delete it once your archive is made.

Settings

MessageArchiver › Settings… (⌘,):

  • Permissions: Full Disk Access and Contacts, with Check Again.
  • Search index: its size and when it was updated; Update Now, or Delete… to move it to the Trash (it's made again when needed).
  • Sources: Messages and each plug-in, with what it reads; Instructions, Remove…, Install Plug-in…, and the plug-ins' page.
  • Converters: what converts photos, audio and video. sips and afconvert come with macOS; ffmpeg is optional.
  • Appearance and skin: light, dark or automatic, and the app's colours. Skins are small JSON files you can export, edit and import.

Privacy and safety

  • Read only. MessageArchiver never changes Messages, its attachments, Contacts, an iPhone backup, or another app's data. A plug-in reads a copy of that app's files.
  • Plug-ins run only if RYSYS signed them and nothing changed since, and each runs with no network access at all.
  • Offline. It makes no network connections. Nothing leaves your Mac; the link at the bottom only opens this page.
  • Never overwrites. A new export is a new folder. An update sends what it replaces to the Trash.
  • Its own files are in ~/Library/Application Support/RYSYS4MAC/MessageArchiver/: settings, your merges and names, the search index (a copy of your messages' text, readable only by you), and Plugins/. Delete that folder, and the app, to remove MessageArchiver completely.

Questions

Why does the count of messages differ from Messages?

Tapbacks (hearts, thumbs up and the like) are shown on the message they react to rather than as messages of their own, and messages in more than one conversation are counted once.

Can I export just one conversation?

Yes: untick everything with the checkbox at the top of the list, tick that conversation, and click Export…

Is it free?

Yes. MessageArchiver is free software under the GNU GPL v3. It's built on imessage-exporter's library by ReagentX. Its source code is at github.com/rysys-dev/MessageArchiver, and the third-party notices are in MessageArchiver › About MessageArchiver.
