Signal

This plug-in lets MessageArchiver read your Signal chats, beside Messages. A person you talk to on both is one conversation, each message marked with how it was sent.

Bring your Signal chats in

  1. Install Signal Desktop and link it to your phone. When it offers to, transfer your message history from the phone.
  2. In Signal Desktop, open Settings (the gear, lower left) ▸ Chats ▸ Export chat history, and press Export. Signal saves a folder named signal-export- and the date, in Downloads.
  3. In MessageArchiver, choose Signal: Choose an Export… from the source menu (or Settings ▸ Sources), and choose that folder, or Downloads itself: the newest export in it is read.

Signal isn’t in iPhone backups, so on a Mac this export is the way in.

Later exports

Make a new export the same way and update your archive. Only what is new is added; a person, a conversation and a message keep the place they have in it. If you chose Downloads, the newest export there is read without choosing again.

What is read

One-to-one chats, groups and Note to Self, with reactions, replies, edits (every version), deleted messages, calls and group changes. Signal’s release notes and stories are left out. Someone Signal knows only by their Signal id, not a phone number, is listed by that id with the name they gave Signal, and isn’t matched to Contacts; you can join them to the right person by hand in People.

Signal Desktop holds only what it has: what it got since it was linked, and what the phone transferred then, which may leave out older photos and files. Those are listed as missing.

Keep the export safe

The export folder holds your messages unencrypted, and Signal’s keys for your account. Once your archive is made, you may want to move the export somewhere safe, or delete it. MessageArchiver never copies the keys into an archive.

What it can’t do

It reads only: nothing in Signal or the export is changed, and it has no network access at all.
